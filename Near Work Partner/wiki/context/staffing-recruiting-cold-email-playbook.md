# Staffing and Recruiting Cold Email Playbook

Use this page with [[context/cold-email-copywriting]] when writing or reviewing outbound copy for Near campaigns.

Core standard: **relevant, specific, credible, useful, human, low-friction, persona-aware, and varied across the sequence.**

## 1. Relevance Beats Personalization

Personalization proves research. Relevance proves why the prospect should care.

Use signals tied to hiring or business needs:
- Open roles.
- Growth or funding.
- Team expansion.
- New markets or service lines.
- Remote or international hiring.
- Hard-to-fill skills.
- Previous engagement.
- Strong candidate availability.
- Compensation or talent-market gaps.

Avoid trivia, generic compliments, and facts that do not connect to the offer.

## 2. One Campaign, One Core Angle

Build each campaign around one primary reason to engage:
- Candidate-led.
- Open role.
- Hiring signal.
- Cost efficiency.
- Talent scarcity.
- Speed.
- Retention.
- Remote expansion.
- Re-engagement.
- Competitor or peer activity.

Do not stack several weak angles into one email.

## 3. Sell the Outcome, Not the Service

Lead with what the prospect wants:
- Fill roles faster.
- Access better talent.
- Reduce hiring costs.
- Improve retention.
- Expand capacity.
- Reach skills unavailable locally.

Near is the mechanism, not the headline.

## 4. Candidate-Led Copy

Candidate-led angles make the offer concrete, lower commitment, and create curiosity.

Lead with:
- Relevant experience.
- Industry background.
- Measurable performance.
- Tools or technical skills.
- Compensation fit.
- Time-zone alignment.

Only imply a candidate exists, is available, or can interview when verified.

Without a confirmed candidate, use market-led language:
- "We regularly recruit..."
- "We tend to see strong talent with..."
- "This is a profile we know well."

## 5. Proof-Matching Hierarchy

Use the closest available proof:

1. Same role and same industry.
2. Same role and similar company.
3. Adjacent role and same industry.
4. Relevant functional experience.
5. Broad company metrics.

One matched proof point beats several generic statistics.

## 6. Open Roles Are A Trigger, Not The Whole Strategy

For job-post campaigns:
- Name the role.
- Reference one meaningful requirement.
- Confirm remote or LatAm viability when possible.
- Avoid summarizing the job description.
- Never assume international openness.

The job post creates timing. The value proposition still has to carry the email.

## 7. Email 1: One-One-One-One

A strong first-touch default:
- One trigger.
- One value proposition.
- One proof point.
- One CTA.

Use this for clarity, not as a rigid rule for every email in the sequence.

## 8. Sequence Variation

Each email needs a different function:
- **Email 1:** establish relevance and value.
- **Email 2:** short nudge or simpler question.
- **Email 3:** new angle, proof, objection, or insight.
- **Final email:** remove pressure and preserve future relevance.

Do not rewrite the same pitch four times.

Variation can come from:
- CTA.
- Proof.
- Pain point.
- Candidate example.
- Compensation insight.
- Case study.
- Timing.
- Referral request.
- Message length.

## 9. Information Discipline

Most emails need:
- One idea.
- One relevant detail.
- One reason to believe.
- One next step.

Remove:
- Company introductions.
- Feature lists.
- Multiple candidates.
- Several metrics.
- Process explanations.
- Multiple questions.
- Competing CTAs.

## 10. Human, Scannable Writing

Use:
- Short sentences.
- Short paragraphs.
- Familiar words.
- Simple punctuation.
- Natural rhythm.
- One visually separate CTA.

Avoid:
- Buzzwords.
- Corporate filler.
- Ego-stroking.
- Dense paragraphs.
- Excessive enthusiasm.
- Overwritten transitions.
- Robotic personalization.

Write like a capable operator, not a marketing workflow.

## 11. Subject Lines

Best default:
- 2-5 words.
- Relevant.
- Specific.
- Non-promotional.
- Similar to internal communication.

Useful formats:
- Role.
- Candidate type.
- Hiring topic.
- Market.
- Operational question.

Internal camouflage can improve opens; deception damages trust.

Avoid:
- "Quick thought."
- "Exciting opportunity."
- "Top talent."
- "Partnership."
- Fake "Re:".

## 12. Opening Lines

The first sentence must establish relevance immediately.

Lead with:
- Hiring signal.
- Business trigger.
- Candidate fit.
- Team change.
- Market insight.
- Previous interaction.

Avoid:
- "Hope you're well."
- "Wanted to introduce myself."
- "Came across your company."
- "Impressed by your growth."
- Long Near introductions.

## 13. CTAs

Ask for the smallest logical next step:
- Send a profile.
- Share examples.
- Compare compensation.
- Confirm timing.
- Test LatAm viability.
- Identify the right owner.
- Discuss the search.

Rules:
- One question.
- Easy to answer.
- Directly connected to the offer.
- Reply-first before calendar-first.

## 14. No-Oriented Questions

No-oriented CTAs can reduce pressure and give the prospect control:
- "Would LatAm be off the table?"
- "Is this no longer a priority?"
- "Would it be unreasonable to compare profiles?"

Use selectively. Repetition turns the technique into a script.

## 15. Quality Before Cost

Position LatAm through:

1. Relevant experience.
2. Communication.
3. Time-zone alignment.
4. Retention.
5. Speed.
6. Cost efficiency.

Cost should support value, not define the candidate.

## 16. Compensation

Use compensation only when it is:
- Accurate.
- Current.
- Role-specific.
- Seniority-aligned.
- Presented in the right monthly or annual format.

Never use unrealistic low-end numbers to manufacture interest.

## 17. Credibility Rules

Never invent or exaggerate:
- Candidate availability.
- Candidate performance.
- Placements.
- Hiring speed.
- Employee locations.
- Savings.
- Industry expertise.
- Company growth.
- Hiring intent.

Specificity works only when true.

## 18. Persona Matching

Match the angle to the buyer:
- **Executives:** growth, speed, flexibility, cost.
- **Hiring managers:** skills, quality, workload, fit.
- **HR/Talent:** pipeline, vetting, process, candidate experience.
- **Finance:** cost, risk, compliance, retention.
- **Operations:** reliability, capacity, execution, coverage.

Same company does not mean same message.

## 19. Pressure And Urgency

Avoid:
- Fake scarcity.
- Guilt.
- Apologetic follow-ups.
- "Final attempt."
- "Stop bothering you."
- "Have you given up?"
- "Just checking in."

Use calm confidence. Withdraw pressure without withdrawing value.

## 20. Optimization

Track downstream quality, not just replies:
- Positive reply rate.
- Positive to booked.
- Booked to qualified.
- Qualified to opportunity.
- Opportunity to hire.
- Negative replies.
- Complaints.

A reply-generating claim that Sales cannot fulfill is losing copy.

## 21. Testing

Test one meaningful variable at a time:
- Candidate-led vs market-led.
- Quality vs savings.
- Direct vs no-oriented CTA.
- Short vs contextual follow-up.
- Proof vs no proof.
- Trigger type.
- Profile vs market-insight offer.

Do not rewrite every variable simultaneously.

## 22. Segmentation

Segment before writing by:
- Persona.
- Role family.
- Seniority.
- Industry.
- Company size.
- Hiring signal.
- Remote readiness.
- International presence.
- Candidate supply.
- Likely pain.

Strong targeting creates strong copy.

## 23. AI Prompt Guardrails

Prompts should separate:
- Verified facts.
- Allowed inferences.
- Optional proof.
- Prohibited claims.

Rules:
- Omit missing data.
- Never fabricate specificity.
- Never present inference as fact.
- Never imply candidate availability unless confirmed.
- Select only the most relevant proof.
- Vary structure across the sequence.

## 24. Final QA

Before sending, confirm:
- Clear reason for outreach.
- Strong, relevant angle.
- Claims verified.
- One main idea.
- CTA aligned with the offer.
- Natural language.
- Right persona.
- Distinct from previous touches.
- Expectations Near can fulfill.
