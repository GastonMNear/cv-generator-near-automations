Read Daniel's "Conversion Improvement Strategy" doc (already in Gaston's email / Google Drive) and
return concrete feedback. It contains the reframed No-HM copy plus a list of **50 underperforming

## What the doc argues
The transversal element in everything that converts is **reaching the actual hiring manager**. The
baseline works on hiring managers regardless of function, and on No-HM CEOs/founders under 50 — and
underperforms everywhere else, because the copy *assumes* the recipient is the manager. In No-HM
that's a coin flip: only ~1.4 people are reached per company.

New copy structure: acknowledge they're hiring → mention the teammates also contacted → include a
job-description snippet to prove relevance → offer candidate profiles → **ask to be pointed to
whoever owns the process**. Honest routing instead of presumption.

## Feedback Gaston already gave (carry into the review)
1. **The teammate mention breaks when only one contact exists** at a company. Daniel accepted: drop
   that line in those cases.
2. **Co-workers has an advantage this doesn't** — it fires only *after* the hiring manager was
   emailed and didn't reply, so the routing ask is earned rather than cold.
3. **Keep the test narrow.** Daniel floated dropping role segmentation entirely; Gaston argued for
   engineering/marketing/sales as the test bed — thousands of leads, known-poor performance, so
   pulling them costs at most one booked call while giving enough volume for significance. Expand to
   the other 47 segments only once these three prove out. Daniel agreed.

**Guardrail both agreed on:** do **not** experiment on hiring managers or on No-HM CEOs/founders
under 50. Those convert and must be protected.
