# Marketing Stand Up — Record August, Reply Inconsistency, AE Qualification Overhaul

## TLDR
- **August was a record month: 291 calls booked.** But a mid-month dip in email replies stopped the
  team from beating the all-time weekly record of 47 calls, set back in March.
- **The binding constraint is reply *consistency*, not volume.** Weeks that drop below 40 positive
  replies drag call volume under the 10-call "endangered territory" benchmark, because conversion
  runs ~20%. That ratio is what turns a reply dip directly into a call dip.
- **Gaston owns the diagnosis:** analyse the last 8 weeks of email replies and find what the
  sub-40 weeks have in common → [[wiki/tasks/2026-08-31-analyze-8-week-reply-patterns]].
- **AE qualification is being overhauled this week** (Franco + Carlos) because high call volume isn't
  converting into *qualified* call volume. Three root causes named: subjective disqualification
  ("lead didn't seem engaged"), CRM outcomes not updated within 24h, and AEs lazily approving
  incorrect AI-suggested outcomes.
- **Reddit tracking moves to karma points** as an objective activity measure, replacing subjective
  "warming up" status reports.

## Key Decisions
- **Positive-reply consistency becomes the focus metric** for cold email — beating the 40/week floor
  matters more than raw send volume. Consistent with the Aug 18 finding that calls-booked needs
  ~40k leads to be statistically readable, so positive replies is the usable metric.
- **New, tighter AE qualification criteria ship this week**, built with Franco and Carlos, explicitly
  to remove subjective judgment from disqualification.
- **Reddit account activity is measured by karma**, not self-report.
- **Financial Times award application: undecided.** Two objections — it requires sharing sensitive
  company information, and every competitor who has won was VC-backed, suggesting bias. Andres
  follows up for a final call.

## Context
Per-person booking numbers were weak against the record month: Felix 2, Sebastian 2 (he prioritised
listicle swaps), Ivan 0 with a stated goal of 1/day this week after his dialer adjustments. The
Friday drop-off after a strong Monday–Thursday was called out as a **recurring pattern**, not a
one-off — which is part of what the 8-week analysis is meant to explain.

The AE qualification problem is a revenue risk rather than a marketing one: the team is generating
calls that don't survive qualification, and the data integrity gap (calls left in "scheduled") means
nobody can measure how bad it is. This is the same class of attribution problem Gaston chased on
Aug 24, from the qualification side rather than the source side.
