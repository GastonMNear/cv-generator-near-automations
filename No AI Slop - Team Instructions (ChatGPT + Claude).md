# No AI Slop: Writing Standards for Near's Marketing Team

Add these instructions to your ChatGPT custom instructions, Claude project, or any AI tool you use for writing. These rules apply to ALL written output: memos, emails, Slack messages, blog posts, social copy, reports.

---

## The Core Rule

**If a sentence doesn't change what someone DOES or DECIDES, delete it.** No sentence gets to exist just because it sounds smart. The bullets you used to prompt the AI are probably more valuable than the polished output. ("I'd rather get those than the slop.")

---

## Banned Words and Phrases

Delete these on sight. Replace with plain language.

| AI Slop | Say Instead |
|---|---|
| Delve into / unpack | Look at / break down |
| This signals that / this underscores | [Just state the cause and effect directly] |
| Navigate the complexities of | [Name the actual complexity] |
| In an ever-changing landscape | [What specifically is changing?] |
| Synergies / leverage our learnings | Here's what worked, here's what didn't |
| Holistic approach | [Say the actual approach] |
| Robust | Strong / solid / [use a number instead] |
| Seamless / seamlessly | [Describe what actually happens] |
| Streamline | Speed up / simplify / cut steps |
| Aligned / alignment | [Say what matches what] |
| Best practices | [Name the specific practice] |
| Stakeholders | [Name the people] |
| Deep dive | Analysis / review |
| Lean into | Do / focus on / invest in |
| Unlock / unlocking | Get / create / enable |
| At the end of the day | [Delete. Just state the point.] |
| Utilize | Use |
| Ensure | Make sure |
| Optimize (vaguely) | [Say what you changed and what improved] |
| Actionable | [Just give the action] |

## 5 Structural Patterns That Scream "AI Wrote This"

### 1. Transition Word Openers
Every paragraph starts with "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Importantly," "Consequently," or "That said."

**Fix:** Read the first word of every paragraph. If 3+ start with transition words, rewrite. Just start with the point.

### 2. Bold:Colon:Explanation Bullets
You've seen this:
- **Clarity:** Ensure your memo is clear and concise.
- **Alignment:** Make sure all stakeholders are on the same page.
- **Execution:** Focus on actionable next steps.

**Fix:** If the text after the colon could apply to any company on earth, it's useless. Replace with specifics: "Write shorter memos. Send to three people max. Include deadlines."

### 3. The Corporate Therapist Voice
"This is a powerful opportunity to lean into our strengths and foster a culture of accountability."

**Fix:** Say what you'd actually say to a coworker. "We're good at X. We need to start doing Y. Here's the plan."

### 4. The Neat Little Bow Conclusion
"Ultimately, the goal is to build a more resilient and agile organization."

**Fix:** If your conclusion could apply to literally any company, it's not a conclusion. End with the specific next action or decision needed.

### 5. The "Says Everything, Means Nothing" Paragraph
You read an entire paragraph, it sounds smart, and then you can't summarize what it actually said.

**Fix:** After writing each paragraph, state its point in one plain sentence. If you can't, the paragraph is pattern-matching language, not thinking. Delete it and use the plain sentence instead.

---

## 3 More Tells

1. **Forced negation:** "Not this, but that." "Instead of X, Y." AI loves this structure because it sounds clever. Just say the second part.

2. **Staccato repetitions:** "Not this. Or this. Or this. But that." Same problem. Just say what you mean.

3. **Excessive adverbs:** "Quietly underscores," "deeply resonates," "fundamentally shifts," "strategically positioned." If removing the adverb doesn't change the meaning, remove it. "This is important" beats "this quietly underscores."

## How to Use AI Without Producing Slop

### DO:
1. **Write your first draft yourself.** Talk it out loud. Use voice dictation. That's your raw material, and it already sounds human because it came from one.

2. **Use AI to improve YOUR draft, not to replace your thinking.** Ask:
   - "What's missing? What questions would a reader still have?"
   - "Where is my language unclear?"
   - "Tighten this. Make it more concise without changing my voice."
   - "Does my argument flow logically?"

3. **Use AI for research, structure, and editing.** It's great at organizing your ideas, finding gaps, and cutting fluff. Let it be the editor, not the author.

### DO NOT:
1. **Paste a topic and ask AI to write the whole thing.** The output will be pattern-matched language, not thinking. It will sound smart and mean nothing.

2. **Accept the first draft from AI.** Always rewrite in your own voice. If you can't explain each paragraph's point in plain English, delete it.

3. **Use AI output without reading every sentence.** Your name goes on it. You're responsible for what it says.

## The Tests (Run These Before Sending Anything)

**Test 1: The Plain English Test.** Can you summarize each paragraph in one sentence? If not, the paragraph is fluff.

**Test 2: The Behavior Test.** Does this sentence change what someone does or decides? If not, delete it.

**Test 3: The Read-Aloud Test.** Read it out loud. If you wouldn't say "Moreover" or "Furthermore" in conversation, don't write it.

**Test 4: The "Any Company" Test.** Could this sentence appear in any company's memo? If yes, it's generic. Replace with your specifics, your numbers, your context.

**Test 5: The First-Word Scan.** Look at the first word of every paragraph. If you see Moreover/Furthermore/Additionally/Importantly more than once, rewrite.

## Quick Reference: What Good Looks Like

**BAD (AI slop):**
"By leveraging our learnings from Q1, we can unlock synergies across teams and take a more holistic approach to customer acquisition. This signals a shift in our strategy and underscores the need for greater alignment across stakeholders."

**GOOD (human):**
"Q1 taught us three things. Cold email doesn't work without a named recruiter. On-site campaigns convert 2x remote. Our best channel is still referrals at 33% close rate. We're doubling down on referrals and testing on-site outbound."

**BAD:**
"Unlocking your true potential starts with embracing a growth mindset and leaning into the discomfort of transformation."

**GOOD:**
"Do more than you currently are, every day." (That's it. That's the whole point. Delete the rest.)
